# Trademark policy

The Standbye **code** is open source under [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). The Standbye **name and
logo** are not — Section 6 of that license grants no trademark rights, and this page says what we
do and don't allow, so you don't have to guess.

## You don't need permission to

- Use, fork, modify, redistribute and sell the code, per the Apache 2.0 license.
- Say truthfully that your project is "built on Standbye", "a fork of Standbye", or "compatible
  with Standbye". Nominative use is fine and we like it.
- Use the name in articles, talks, tutorials, reviews and comparisons.
- Keep the name and logo in an **unmodified** build you redistribute.

## Please ask first before

- Naming a **modified** build, fork or derivative "Standbye", or anything close enough to be
  confused with it (Standbye Pro, StandbyeAI, Standby, and so on).
- Using the logo or wordmark as the icon or branding of your own product or service.
- Registering a domain, social handle, app-store listing or package name containing "Standbye".
- Implying that your product, service or fork is endorsed by, affiliated with, or an official
  distribution of Standbye.

## Why this exists

Anyone may run their own build of this code — that's the point of an open license. What the
trademark protects is a narrow thing: that when someone downloads something called **Standbye**,
or visits **standbye.navid.tr**, they get the software we actually maintain. A fork that ships under
its own name is welcome. A fork that ships under ours is a support burden for us and a confusing
download for the user.

Questions, or want permission for something on the second list? Open an issue or write to
**hello@standbye.org**.
